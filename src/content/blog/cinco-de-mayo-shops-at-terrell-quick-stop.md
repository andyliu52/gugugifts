## Cinco de Mayo on a Tuesday Doesn't Have to Be Boring

Cinco de Mayo 2026 falls on **Tuesday, May 5** — which means most of you reading this aren't planning a Saturday-night blowout. You're working through it. But there's still margin in the day for a smart 30-minute mid-week stop, and **The Shops at Terrell** (still mostly known by its old name, Tanger Outlets Terrell) is the Kaufman County spot for it.

Here's a quick lunchtime gameplan if you find yourself near I-20 Exit 501 today.

## The 30-Minute Loop

**12:00 — Park.** Free lot, biggest cluster near Nike and the food trucks. Choose anywhere on the main loop and you're 200 feet from everything.

**12:05 — Anchor stop.** If you actually need something, hit your one anchor: Old Navy for kids' uniforms, Levi's for jeans, Famous Footwear for sneakers. Get in, get out.

**12:20 — Lunch.** Rocky Mountain Chocolate is a classic detour for a small treat. The center has rotating restaurants and a Hometown Cinema if you've got more time than a true 30-minute window.

**12:30 — Gugu Gifts (Suite 112).** This is the move. If you're going to a Cinco de Mayo gathering tonight or this weekend, walking in with a curated little gift is the difference between "you came" and "you really came."

**12:40 — Back on I-20** with the gift wrapped, ready for whatever your evening looks like.

## Why Mid-Week Stops Beat Saturday Stops

Saturdays at any retail center in north Texas are a different world. Mid-week — especially around lunch — the lot is half-empty, the staff has time to actually talk to you, and you don't waste 20 minutes parking for a 5-minute purchase. If you live in Forney, Mesquite, Heartland, Travis Ranch, or anywhere in Kaufman County, you already know this trade-off. Today is the day to use it.
